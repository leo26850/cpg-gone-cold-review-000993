CPG Affiliate · copy review

Gone Cold — the full sequences

Three sequences, 26 emails each, one email every two weeks for twelve months. Built from the 31 July call with Krik, Kas, Leonardo and Julieta.

78 emails ~700 contacts addressable now Plane epic CPG-312 v2 · supersedes the 28 Jul draft

Krik's brief, verbatim: "I just never want clients ever going cold. I never want us to stop emailing them unless they tell me to f off... I want to, for years on, email the same guy."

1 · Legacy 380 2 · General pipeline 3 · Booking drop-off Voice Open items

How the three differ

Split by how the person went cold — that determines what context exists and what the copy can honestly reference.

SeqWhoWhat the copy doesSize
1Never replied, or said no. 6-24 months cold. Reopens an old no whose reason has expired.~380
2Met us, then went quiet. Ongoing. Names what they said last time and answers it.~170+
3Replied to cold email, never booked. Removes booking friction. Points at the calendar.~180+

Krik kept them as separate workflows deliberately — "so I could see who's in the cold email one, who's in the normal one." Reporting hygiene, and it makes the copy comparable.

Stop rules apply to all three. The prospect's first reply kills their sequence dead and hands over to Julieta. Opt-out is permanent and blocks re-entry to every sequence. Nothing here re-sends to someone who has answered.

The voice

Krik's brief: "Make them short, make them punchy and make them salesy, but not cheesy, but just hit the main points. Yo, what's the risk? ... you're probably losing money on Meta anyway, dealing with fluctuating CACs. You're always looking for a new customer acquisition channel. Short, punchy sales things — they're hooks. They're all good hooks."

Applied as: 40-90 words · one hook · one ask · no "just circling back" · one question mark · no emoji · signed with a first name. Every email is hook, one line of substance, one ask.
