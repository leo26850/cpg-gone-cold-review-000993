Gone Cold Revival Sequence

Thirteen emails for the brand-side nurture lane: seven trigger emails and six follow-ups. Fills the copy gap left open at section C of the proposal base deck copy, where the sequence currently stops at an internal handoff note.

Drafted 2026-07-28 Status draft for Kas review Plane CPG-180, CPG-104 Voice Krik, first person Evidence 199 cold-cohort calls 7 triggers + 6 follow-ups

Everything below is written from the CPG Fireflies corpus rather than from doctrine alone. The objection and pain rankings come from cpg-data-warehouse.cpg.fireflies_calls restricted to the cold cohort; the verbatim quotes come from thirteen full transcripts of stalled deals between 2025-08 and 2026-02, pulled through the Fireflies API and read end to end.

Still missing: the CPG team's sent-email corpus. The deck copy doc flags that no such corpus exists to calibrate against, and that is still true. Every email below is derived from spoken language on calls, not from what has actually been sent and replied to.

01The governing principle

Trigger-based, never time-based.

Section 6.2 of the 2-call SOP is explicit about the nurture lane: one email every 2 to 4 weeks, “each anchored to a REAL trigger. No trigger, no email.”

That is the single biggest difference from section C. Touches 1 through 3 fire on a clock. Everything here fires on an event. If no event has occurred, the correct number of emails to send is zero. The only clock-driven email in the set is E7, and it fires at most once every twelve months.

So the sequence has no fixed length. A deal can sit in the lane for a year and receive two emails, or receive four in a quarter because their season landed and their Meta costs moved. Both are correct.

03Entry, exit, suppression

EntryOnly after the break-up touch has been sent and gone unanswered. Attio label stays nurture network lead. The words “gone cold lead” never appear on a brand-side record, so the title of this page is internal vocabulary only.
Exit, upwardAny reply flips the deal back to active and is answered by Krik personally, not by the next scheduled touch.
Never enters“Send me the deck” plus “not sure there's a fit” (archive). Deck plus references plus “contact me end of year” (downshift). Silent or monosyllabic right after price (soft-DQ). Anti-ICP or hard-gated. New: concessions already stacked before the deal died. A waiver rescues an energy match, not a dead deal. Those get E3 and E7 only.
Cadence ceilingOne email per 4 weeks no matter how many triggers fire. If a season and a Meta spike land in the same fortnight, send E1 and hold E2. Two triggers do not license two emails.
Hard stopThree consecutive trigger-anchored emails with zero opens drops the deal to E7-only. Any explicit opt-out ends everything immediately.

04Follow-up rules

Each trigger email gets at most one follow-up, shown indented beneath its parent.

Judgement call that needs a ruling. A follow-up strains two doctrines: “no trigger, no email” says a second email needs a second event, and the neediness doctrine caps chasing, in a lane that already sits after a break-up. The resolution below is that a follow-up is not a new touch, it is the second half of the same touch. That is my reading, not an established rule. If it reads the other way, keep F4 and F5 and cut the rest; those are the two where a non-reply is genuinely ambiguous rather than a soft no.
One per trigger, everThere is no second follow-up. If it goes unanswered the deal returns to dormant and waits for the next genuine trigger. No third touch on the same event.
Threaded, never newReply inside the parent's thread with “Re:”. A new thread reads as a new campaign and destroys the “I only reach out when it is useful” positioning that E3 spends its whole body earning.
Timing7 to 10 days after the parent [DRAFT-CADENCE], except F4 at 5 to 7 days because it carries the highest intent and its window is one the prospect set themselves.
Cadence ceilingThe 4-week ceiling counts triggers, not emails, so a parent plus its follow-up is one unit against it. The pair counts as one touch for the hard stop.
Always shorterEvery follow-up is under 90 words. A follow-up longer than what it follows is a re-pitch.
Never repeatEach one advances something: a number, an artifact, a lower-cost ask, or a question that lets them close the loop honestly. Repetition is what makes chasing feel like chasing.

05The emails

Seven triggers, six follow-ups. Production notes are internal and never rendered to a prospect.

E1

Their season is approaching

Trigger: a real, named seasonal spike for this brand is 4 to 8 weeks out [DRAFT-CADENCE]. Their season, not a generic retail calendar.

Subject{{seasonal_trigger}} is close
Hi {{first_name}}, {{seasonal_trigger}} is about {{weeks_out}} weeks out, and when we spoke you told me it is one of the spikes that actually moves the year for {{brand_name}}. We are lining up publisher inventory for that window now. If you want me to hold a slot for {{brand_name}}, tell me and I will put one aside. If the timing is still wrong, that is genuinely fine, and I will watch for the next one. Everything we scoped stays true: {{proposed_CPA}} per verified new customer, benchmarked below your Meta CAC, and live about two weeks from paperwork. Krik
Production notes 108 words
The strongest trigger in the corpus, because it is the prospect's own stated commercial rhythm rather than our calendar, and because Mora already runs the identical motion for live clients. {{seasonal_trigger}} must be real and specific to the brand; a generic “Q4 is coming” breaks the no-fake-urgency rule and should fail the human gate. The slot-hold is a real scarcity statement only if inventory is genuinely being allocated. If it is not, cut that paragraph rather than invent it.
F1 · follows E1

The allocation date

Fires: 7 to 10 days after E1, and only while the season is still at least 2 weeks out. If the window has closed, send nothing and wait for next year.

SubjectRe: {{seasonal_trigger}} is close
Hi {{first_name}}, Quick one. We allocate the {{seasonal_trigger}} inventory on {{allocation_date}}, so I would rather ask before that closes than after. If {{brand_name}} wants a slot, one line back is enough and I will handle the rest. If not, I will stop asking about this one. Krik
Production notes 58 words
The deadline is real or this email does not send; an invented allocation date is exactly the fake urgency the global rules ban. “I will stop asking about this one” is the pressure valve, and it is a promise that must be kept, meaning no second follow-up on the same season.
E2

Their Meta costs moved

Trigger: verifiable evidence that paid-social acquisition cost has deteriorated for this brand or its category. Research agent proposes, Kas verifies before send.

SubjectYour Meta number
Hi {{first_name}}, Not selling you anything today. {{meta_cost_evidence}}, and when we spoke your blended CAC was {{their_CAC}}. The reason I do not run Meta or Google is that every brand is already in that auction, so when it gets expensive there is nowhere to hide. What we run is placements you are not in yet, priced per verified new customer instead of per impression. If your number has moved since {{call_date}}, that is worth fifteen minutes: {{krik_calendar_link}} If it has not, ignore me and have a strong quarter. Krik
Production notes 106 words
Built on Krik's verbatim differentiation and his benchmark discipline. {{meta_cost_evidence}} must be a checkable claim, not a vibe. The permitted guarantee shape appears here as a RATE benchmarked to their CAC and nothing more. The closing line is the abundance posture doing real work: it gives them a costless exit, which is what keeps the door open for E1 later.
F2 · follows E2

The arithmetic

Fires: 7 to 10 days after E2. E2 makes the argument, F2 puts the number on it.

SubjectRe: Your Meta number
Hi {{first_name}}, One number and then I will leave it. When we spoke your blended CAC was {{their_CAC}}. I benchmark against that and come in 10 to 15 percent under, which puts {{brand_name}} at roughly {{proposed_CPA}} per verified new customer. The budget behind it is your money, protected in a middleman account, and it only spends when it produces those customers. If that is worth fifteen minutes: {{krik_calendar_link}} Krik
Production notes 81 words
The escalation is deliberate. E2 argues the shape, F2 commits to the arithmetic, which is Krik's verbatim pricing logic (“I typically benchmark versus meta and I go like 10, 15% lower than meta”). Escrow reframe is carried in full because the budget is named. The guarantee stays a RATE benchmarked to their own CAC, which is the only permitted form.
E3

A publisher worth knowing about, no ask

Trigger: a genuinely relevant publisher or placement has entered the network since the deal went cold. The only email safe to send to a concessions-stacked deal, because it asks for nothing.

SubjectSomething for {{brand_name}}, no reply needed
Hi {{first_name}}, No response needed on this one. We brought {{publisher_descriptor}} into the network this month, and it is the placement I had in mind for {{brand_name}} when we spoke. {{publisher_rationale}} I am not asking you for anything. I said I would only reach out when there was something genuinely useful, and this is the first thing that cleared that bar. If you ever want to look at it, you know where I am. Krik
Production notes 82 words, zero asks
Direct descendant of Touch 3's value-drop logic and of the break-up promise (“I will only reach out from here when we have something genuinely useful”), which this email explicitly honours. That callback is the point: it proves the break-up email was true, which is what buys credibility for E1 and E2. No named brands unless they are on the 9-name whitelist.
F3 · follows E3Engagement-gated

Permission to close

Fires: only if the prospect opened E3 more than once or clicked. On no engagement, send nothing. E3 promised “no response needed”, and chasing a reply to it breaks the promise the email was built on.

SubjectRe: Something for {{brand_name}}, no reply needed
Hi {{first_name}}, You had a look at the {{publisher_descriptor}} note, so I will ask one question and then leave it alone. Is affiliate something you are revisiting for {{brand_name}} this year, or should I stop sending these? Either answer is genuinely useful to me. Krik
Production notes 54 words
A permission-to-close question, which converts a non-answer into a real one and gives them a dignified way to end it. “Either answer is genuinely useful” has to be true in practice: a “stop” reply moves the record to E7-only immediately, and if it does not, this email is a lie.
E4

The budget cycle they named has come around

Trigger: the prospect stated a return window on the call and it has arrived. The 6-month timer for frozen-budget and tariff deals lands here too.

SubjectYou said a couple of months
Hi {{first_name}}, When we spoke on {{call_date}} you were straight with me. The budget was not there right then, and you said to come back in {{stated_return_window}}. This is me doing that. Nothing has changed on our side. The setup fee is the only fee that exists. The budget is not a fee either, it is your money, protected in a middleman account, and it only spends when it generates what we agreed. Whatever goes unused stays refundable. If the picture has changed for {{brand_name}}, fifteen minutes and we pick it straight back up: {{krik_calendar_link}} Krik
Production notes 117 words
The highest-intent email in the lane and the one with the least room for cleverness, because the prospect wrote the brief himself. Quoting their own window back is the entire mechanism, so {{stated_return_window}} must be their words. Full escrow reframe is present because money is mentioned. Tier numbers stay out of the email, per the section B precedent.
F4 · follows E4

Rather know than wonder

Fires: 5 to 7 days after E4, the tightest gap in the lane, because the prospect set this window themselves and intent decays fast.

SubjectRe: You said a couple of months
Hi {{first_name}}, Following up once on this. If the budget still is not there, tell me and I will set a reminder for {{next_window}} instead of guessing. If it is there and the hold-up is something else, tell me that instead and I will deal with it directly. Either way I would rather know than wonder. Krik
Production notes 66 words
Offers nothing and asks for nothing except information, which is why it is safe to send to a deal that died on money. Both branches are useful: one reschedules the trigger honestly, the other surfaces the real objection, which was never budget. “I would rather know than wonder” is the abundance posture in its shortest available form.
E5

The internal blocker has moved

Trigger: evidence the stall was structural rather than commercial. A named absent decision-maker changed role, the champion resurfaced, or the notes recorded an explicit internal stall. Do not fire on a guess.

SubjectWorth another run with {{absent_decision_maker}}?
Hi {{first_name}}, Last time, this stalled internally. That happens constantly and it is nobody's fault. If it would help, I am happy to run the whole thing again with {{absent_decision_maker}} in the room. Thirty minutes, no new document needed. I am truthfully going to try to gain about 2 percent of your DTC revenue here, so it is worth whoever owns the P&L hearing it directly rather than reading it secondhand. If the answer is still no, just say so and I will leave it with you. {{krik_calendar_link}} Krik
Production notes 103 words
Carries Krik's verbatim “about 2 percent of your DTC revenue” line, already load-bearing in Touch 1 and consistent with bounded ambition. Targets the 76 cold-cohort calls tagged not-decision-maker and the SOP's finding that 28 percent of deals die on internal approval. “No new document needed” removes the cost of re-engaging, which is the actual barrier for a champion who already spent capital once. Never re-send the original proposal as the nudge.
F5 · follows E5

Remove the meeting

Fires: 7 to 10 days after E5. E5 asks for a meeting, F5 takes the meeting away.

SubjectRe: Worth another run with {{absent_decision_maker}}?
Hi {{first_name}}, Thought about this differently. Rather than ask you to get {{absent_decision_maker}} onto a call, would it help if I sent you one page you can forward internally? The numbers we discussed, the targets, and what the first three months look like, written for someone who was not on any of our calls. No meeting needed. Say the word and it is with you tomorrow. Krik
Production notes 76 words
The strongest follow-up in the set, because it correctly diagnoses the barrier. The champion is not avoiding us, they are avoiding the internal cost of re-opening a dead thread. Handing them something forwardable moves that cost off their desk. Supported by SOP 6.3: “the proposal's exec summary is built to stand alone for the absent reader.” The one page must actually exist before this sends. “First three months” tracks the three-month minimum term; do not write “90-day launch window”, that branding is not approved.
E6

Proof against the objection that killed it

Trigger: a whitelisted case or shippable artifact now answers the specific objection on the dead deal. Attribution is the highest-volume use at 121 calls.

SubjectOn the {{objection_theme}} question you raised
Hi {{first_name}}, You raised {{their_stated_concern}} when we spoke. It was a fair challenge and I did not have a clean answer for you at the time. I do now. {{proof_artifact}} Short version: we bill only on verified net new customers, we match on view-through so nobody else can claim what we drove, and you watch all of it on a live dashboard in real time instead of waiting on a monthly report. If that changes the maths for {{brand_name}}, I am around: {{krik_calendar_link}} Krik
Production notes 108 words
“I did not have a clean answer at the time” is the one moment of concession in the lane and it is deliberate: it is the highest-trust move available against a trust objection, and it earns the right to re-open. It must be TRUE for the specific deal, so the human gate checks the call notes before this sends. {{proof_artifact}} is whitelist-only; Audien is the established attribution case. The three-part short version is Krik's own corpus language on attribution, compressed.
F6 · follows E6

Offer to show it

Fires: 7 to 10 days after E6. E6 explains, F6 offers to show.

SubjectRe: On the {{objection_theme}} question you raised
Hi {{first_name}}, Last one on this. If reading about it is less useful than seeing it, I can record a short walkthrough of the live dashboard with {{brand_name}} numbers modelled in rather than somebody else's. Five minutes to watch, none to arrange. Want me to make it? Krik
Production notes 57 words
“Last one on this” is literal and binding. The single question ending is the lowest-cost ask available: a one-word reply is a complete answer. Do not record the Loom before they say yes, since the offer is the ask and building it first inverts the economics of the email.
E7

The dormant one-liner

Trigger: none. At most once per 12 months for deals with no other trigger, and the terminal state for hard-stopped deals. The only clock-driven email here.

SubjectStill here
Hi {{first_name}}, Once a year I send a short note to the people I enjoyed talking to, with nothing attached to it. {{brand_name}} is on that list. If affiliate ever comes back onto your roadmap, you know where I am. Hope it is a strong year. Krik
Production notes 52 words
Costs nothing, ages well, and keeps the address warm without ever becoming the thing that gets Krik marked as spam. No link, no calendar, no artifact, deliberately. If this is the only email a deal ever gets again, the relationship still ends in a good place.
F7 · follows E7. None, by design.
E7 is a 52-word annual note with no link, no artifact and no ask. There is nothing to follow up on, and a chase attached to it would convert the one email in the lane that costs the relationship nothing into the one that costs it something. A deal that does not answer E7 gets E7 again next year. If a follow-up is wanted here anyway, the honest version is not a follow-up at all, it is a decision to move the record out of the lane and stop emailing.

07Open items for Kas

  1. Trigger detection is unbuilt. E1, E2, E3 and E6 all assume something is watching for the trigger. Nothing is. Until CPG-180 automates it, this lane runs off a manual monthly review of the nurture list, which is the honest status and should be resourced as such rather than assumed away.
  2. E4 needs a field that does not exist. {{stated_return_window}} has to be captured on the call and written to Attio at the moment the deal dies. No current field holds it. Without it E4 cannot fire, and it is the highest-intent email here.
  3. [DRAFT-CADENCE] on E1's 4 to 8 week window and E7's 12 months. Both inferred. The corpus gives one data point on lead time, Collin Phelan's “two weeks from now”, which is shorter than what E1 assumes.
  4. Vocabulary conflict, needs a ruling. SOP 6.2 bans “gone cold” on brand-side records in favour of “nurture network lead”, but the recruitment pipeline uses gone_cold as a formal stage. This keeps “gone cold” as internal vocabulary only. Confirm before anything ships into Attio.
  5. Suppression against live cold-email campaigns. A prospect in this lane may also sit in an Instantly or Bison sequence. Nothing currently prevents both firing in the same week. Needs a cross-check before go-live.
  6. Ruling needed on whether follow-ups are permitted at all. Section 04 argues a follow-up is the second half of one touch rather than a new one, which keeps “no trigger, no email” intact. That is a reading, not an established rule, and it is the one judgement call here that could be wrong on doctrine rather than on detail. If it reads the other way, keep F4 and F5 and cut the rest.
  7. F3 needs open and click tracking that may not be wired. It is gated on engagement with E3, so if the sending stack does not report opens and clicks back to Attio, F3 cannot fire safely and should be cut rather than sent blind.
  8. F5 promises a one-pager that does not exist. The forwardable internal page has to be built before F5 can send. It is close to the parked case one-pager backlog item in build plan section 5 and could share that work.
